Local research demo · synthetic heldout worlds · zero-shot Chronos-2

Forecast pressure. Treat rider price as the decision.

A foundation model forecasts next-hour potential rider arrivals and service capacity jointly. A transparent economic model then maps candidate rider prices into quoted ETA and conversion. Price itself is not the forecast target.

Observed result: HGB (domain-trained) has the lowest average arrival/capacity MAE across the 64 fixed heldout cases (5.38 count units). Zero-shot Chronos-2 scores 5.40. These are synthetic results, not proof of improvement at Uber.

1. Forecast the state with limited context

First 16 unseen synthetic trajectories, four prespecified origins each, 256 hours of history, one-hour horizon. Chronos receives two target series plus known calendar features with zero task-specific training. Ridge and gradient boosting receive 12,032 labeled examples from the first 32 training trajectories; their hyperparameters are fixed, not tuned on these 64 cases.

2. Map each candidate price to outcomes

All forecasters use the same known current queue and the same fixed heterogeneous price/time sensitivities. The navy curve uses realized synthetic arrival/capacity inputs in this new illustrative calculator; it is not an observed conversion or ETA label. Higher price can reduce requests and quoted congestion without increasing completed conversion when capacity is fixed.

Response errors average all 11 price candidates per case. Grid rule: maximize new completed rides subject to modeled ETA ≤10 minutes; ties choose the lowest price. No feasible action means abstention. Regret uses only 27 identical cases where every model’s selected action is feasible under realized inputs; constraint violations are reported separately. The reference is the best feasible action on this one-step illustrative grid, not a globally optimal price.

What this demonstrates—and what it does not

This is a scrappy architecture demonstration: forecast → explicit economic response → constrained action → validation. The demand/time coefficients are illustrative priors, capacity is exogenous, and neither treatment effects nor Uber One behavior are identified. Multi-target forecasts do not provide a calibrated joint uncertainty distribution. A production system would require internal quote/request/acceptance/pickup data, calibrated uncertainty, pricing experiments, guardrails, and engineering reliability.

Science owns the assumptions, objective, counterfactual validation, and failure analysis. Engineering and science jointly own data contracts, latency budgets, safe serving, experiment rollout, and monitoring. The demo measures plug-in forecast error; it does not recommend deploying a new pricing policy.

Secondary evidence: real NYC rider-fare forecasting benchmark. That is a different target. Its synthetic-fare LoRA checkpoints are not used here and provide no evidence of transfer to arrival, ETA, or conversion prediction.
